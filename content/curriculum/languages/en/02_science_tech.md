# Science & Aerospace English

## 1. Rocket Propulsion & Orbital Dynamics

In aerospace engineering, communication relies on precise terminology: *thrust* (empuje), *payload capacity* (capacidad de carga útil), *apogee* and *perigee* (apogeo y perigeo), *trajectory* and *escape velocity* ($v_{\text{esc}}$).

> [!NOTE] 🤯 Dato WOW
> The term **"T-minus"** denotes the countdown time remaining before scheduled rocket liftoff (e.g., *"T-minus 10 seconds and counting"*).

## 2. Telescopes, Optics & Spectral Analysis

Astronomy papers utilize specific verbs and nouns: *light-gathering power*, *spectroscopy* (análisis espectral), *redshift/blueshift*, *wavelength* (longitud de onda), and *signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)*.

> [!NOTE] 🤯 Dato WOW
> **"Grounded data"** in computer science and astronomy refers to empirical facts backed by physical measurements rather than unverified simulations.

---

## 3. Practical Dialogue: Technical Briefing

Engineers constantly report telemetry status during pre-launch procedures:
- *"All telemetry streams are nominal."* (Todos los flujos de telemetría están dentro de lo normal).
- *"We have a go for main engine ignition."* (Tenemos luz verde para el encendido del motor principal).

> [!TIP] 🚀 Actualidad Científica 2026
> International aerospace consortiums in 2026 mandate standardized English for all joint mission operations between NASA, ESA, and JAXA.

> [!CASE] Dialogue in Context
> - *Flight Director:* "Telemetry Lead, what is the status of the solar array deployment?"
> - *Lead Engineer:* "Solar arrays are fully deployed and locked in position. Power generation is at 104% of nominal."
